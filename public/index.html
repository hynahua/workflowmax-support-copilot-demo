        Worked examples (optional rules practice)
        
Illustrative expected behavior for three fictional samples. These are learning references, not saved live runs, completed reviews or measured outcomes.

        

          
Create a job · T-001

          
Request: “How do I create a job for a client?”

          
Expected: A supported how-to draft using Create a job from scratch (K-001).

          
Reviewer check: Compare the draft with the source and its conditions: every job needs a client, and guidance does not authorise changing customer records. Edit or reject unsupported details before explicitly approving the reply. Approval here records a decision; it does not create a job or send an email.

        

        

          
Job access · T-006

          
Request: “I don’t have permission to view the required job.”

          
Expected: Human handling for the access restriction. The curated quick-time guidance (K-003) directs the user to an administrator or support.

          
Reviewer check: Keep the permission boundary. Do not invent bypass steps, grant access or claim the restriction has been removed.

        

        

          
How-to question plus cancellation signal · T-009

          
Request: “How do I create a job? … We are considering cancelling our subscription. Please have a person contact us.”

          
Expected: Preserve the how-to question, complaint and cancellation signal together, then hand off to a person. A relevant job article does not make this a simple how-to case.

          
Reviewer check: Review the whole message and the requested human follow-up. Do not approve a generic setup answer as though it resolves the complaint or cancellation concern.

        

        
To save an actual rehearsal review, select the matching sample in Optional rules practice, run the rehearsal, and record your own review decision. These examples do not add records to the list above.

      

    


    
A QUICK GUIDE FOR THE REVIEWER
Keep the judgment with a person.
Use this workflow as an assistant, with visible boundaries and a clear handoff.
01
Read the whole request
Look for overlapping intentions. An operation question can also contain a bug report, complaint or cancellation signal.
02
Check evidence and conditions
Open the source, check applicability and compare the draft with the selected facts. Schema and citation checks do not establish factual support.
03
Edit or hand off
Use human handling for uncertainty, missing evidence, permissions, suspected bugs and financial decisions. Do not promise an unverified fix.
04
Record what changed
Save the review decision and reason. A knowledge proposal waits for content-owner approval and later regression checks; one edit does not train the system.
Interview prototype only. All tickets are synthetic. The application never sends customer email. Live-model results and deterministic rehearsals must be reported separately.
